The problem

The dataset is built to fool keyword search.

Four kinds of trap sit in the pool. Lighthouse reasons about the gap between what the JD says and what it means, so each one is handled for the right reason.

🔊

Keyword-stuffers

Profiles that name-drop every term in the JD without the trajectory to back it up.

→ role coherence + career evidence
👯

Behavioral twins

Near-identical on paper; separated by a behavioral modifier reading real signals.

→ behavioral modifier
🪤

~80 honeypots

Subtly impossible histories — tenure longer than the company has existed.

→ explainable honeypot filter
💬

Plain-language fits

Strong candidates who simply don't speak in keywords — still surfaced.

→ semantic fit
How it scores

Five components, gated and grounded.

Every candidate is scored on five explainable components, combined with the JD-derived weights, multiplied by hard-negative gates and a behavioral modifier, with honeypots zeroed — then explained.

Try it live

Rank a batch of candidates.

Use the preloaded 100-candidate sample or upload your own JSONL (≤100). The bge-small encoder runs at request time on CPU — a few seconds — then the full ranker scores, gates, and explains every row.

Note on the sample: this 100-candidate batch is a deliberate stress test — about half are real AI/ML engineers, the rest are traps (non-technical roles with stuffed keywords, honeypots, services-only, off-location). Watch the gates demote the traps to the bottom and zero out honeypots. The official submission (top 100 of 100,000) contains zero traps; see the headline evidence on the README.
